# /apex: do it right the first time

Accuracy over speed. Always. When /apex is invoked, read this whole page and follow every step. It's a contract, not a suggestion.

## 1. Understand before you touch anything
- Open and read everything involved (files, records, emails, data) before you answer. Never guess about something you haven't looked at.
- Work out the whole picture: what exists now, how it's usually done here, what else your change could affect.
- If anything could mean two things, ask now. A wrong assumption gets more expensive with every step.

## 2. Think before you act
- Come up with **2–3 different ways** to do it.
- For each one, give the upside, the risk, and what could go wrong later.
- Pick the **right** one, not the fastest or the easiest, and say why in one or two sentences. If you can't explain the choice, think again.

## 3. Do exactly what was asked
- Follow how things are already done here (names, formats, folders, style).
- Change only what's needed. No bonus changes, no "while I was in there" edits.
- Fix the real cause, not just what's visible on the surface. Never fake a result or fill in made-up numbers.

## 4. Check your own work
- Re-read everything you changed, line by line or record by record.
- Read it back from where it lives (the file, the system, the sheet). Confirm the new value is really there.
- Compare counts and totals before and after. They must match the plan.
- Try the awkward cases: empty, duplicate, missing, the biggest one, the oldest one.
- Ask yourself: "Does this actually solve the problem, or does it just look like it does?" If you're unsure, keep checking. Don't hand over doubt.

## 5. Final check, then report
- Confirm nothing that worked before is broken now.
- Report in plain words:
  - **What I did**, and which approach I chose and why
  - **What changed** (files, records, amounts, counts)
  - **How I checked it**, with the proof
  - **Anything that failed, was skipped, or still needs you**
- If something couldn't be checked, say **NOT CHECKED**. Never say "done" without proof.

Big or risky jobs: do a small batch first (3–5), check it, then the rest. Nothing gets sent, paid, deleted or posted without the user's "yes" for that exact action.
